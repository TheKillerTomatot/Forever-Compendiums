What makes a path a path?

Each dossier answers five practical questions: What is the character’s actual job? What makes a decision different? What resource is being spent? Which form, item and spell support that job? What opportunity disappears because those 51 talent points went elsewhere?

The names are original editorial identities. The talents are checked plans. Neither a name nor a heatmap score is a declaration of raid-tier dominance.

Reading the evidence marks

OFFICIAL identifies published Blizzard notes; CALCULATOR identifies the Forever talent/tree and tooltip references; EDITORIAL identifies qualitative priorities and novel archetypes; UNVERIFIED identifies inaccessible in-game behavior or unanswered acquisition questions.

Source numbers such as [02] lead to the evidence archive, which includes dates and limitations. This guide was compiled 13 November 2026; it does not update itself while offline.

The central promise of this volume

A Cat and a Bear are not the same combat character with different art. A Balance caster without Moonkin is not automatically a weaker version of a Moonkin when the group needs immediate healing. Two Restoration Druids can carry the same spells while holding fundamentally different assignments. The nine paths are built to make those differences visible.

CHAPTER 01 Forms & economies
THE FOREVER DRUID / FIELD GUIDE

The Anatomy of a Shapeshifter

The right body answers the immediate problem. The wrong body turns every resource advantage into a cost.

❈
BODY I · CASTER

Mana, distance and opportunity

In caster/humanoid form, Wrath, Starfire, Moonfire, Insect Swarm where talented, roots and healing all compete for Mana and cast time. Cheap Wrath changes the filler, while paid-for Starfire, Wild Growth and high-risk hardcasts must fit a safe window.

Restoration’s Reflection, Nature’s Swiftness and cast-speed talents can alter the plan. Moonkin allows all non-healing spells but disables heals until you exit.

BODY II · CAT

Energy, position, combo points

Cat is a positional melee character with regenerated Energy, combo-point builders and target-linked finishing decisions. Poised reduces the next Energy ability cost when at full Energy out of combat. Bite, Rip and control compete for the same combo investment.

Shifting Power is a Mana → Energy exchange, not a free permanent resource bar; Furor stores the last Cat Energy through other forms, with specific Bear-form regeneration limitations.

BODY III · BEAR

Rage, threat, incoming blows

Bear and Dire Bear generate Rage through interaction with combat. Your attention belongs to enemy facing, targeted threat, defensive cooldowns, healer survival and interrupt windows. Lacerate is a Feral talent, not a default spell on every Bear.

Swipe strikes three targets; four enemies require control or focused additional threat. Armor, Stamina and Defense matter much more than a Cat’s finisher math.

THE ANATOMY OF A SHAPESHIFTER

Four economies, one character

A form is not a permanent specialization. It establishes which buttons can be used, which resources are active, what position is safe and what the player gives up while in it. Hybrid skill is resource preservation across transitions rather than frantic shape cycling.

Forever makes shifting less punitive in some ways – potions, racials, weapon effects, interactions – but moving between bodies still costs Mana, incurs action opportunity cost and can leave an ally unhealed or an enemy untanked. [01] [06]

Each Wrath supplies faster upcoming Starfires under the current Eclipse talent. Up to four charges can be stored; the effect expires rather than remaining banked forever. This is a decision schematic, not a claim about ideal or measurable second-by-second DPS. [01] [05]

Why Wrath still matters without Eclipse

Forever’s redesigned Wrath is deliberately cheap, especially with Improved Wrath and Moonglow. It is valuable when moving, when you need to avoid an expensive incomplete Starfire, or during a long fight that threatens the Mana pool. Blizzard explicitly says Wrath does not consume Omen of Clarity’s Clearcasting – saving Omen for a more valuable eligible ability is therefore a real consideration. [01]

Do not build a Retail-style solar/lunar energy bar. The Forever Eclipse text is a Wrath → faster-Starfire interaction, not a phase roulette.

Moonkin is a stance, not a healing spec

Moonkin gives armor, protection from Polymorph, doubled chance to trigger Omen and a crit aura to nearby party/raid members. It casts all non-healing spells, not all spells. Emergency healing requires dropping the form, exposing a Mana and timing cost before the heal can begin. The crit aura does not stack with Leader of the Pack. [01] [02]

The Restokin’s absence of Moonkin is therefore not simply -armor or -crit: it allows immediate access to the Restoration spellbook.

Decision tree · what to cast next?
Someone is dying?

Restokin: prepared Swiftmend or Nature’s Swiftness. Moonkin: shift out first, then heal; only if that delay is acceptable.

Movement imminent?

Favor an instant Moonfire or a shorter Wrath window; do not start Starfire if the full cast cannot finish safely.

Eclipse talent?

Weaver: prepare charges with Wrath, then spend fast Starfire before expiration. Lunar: no charges exist.

Several enemies?

Artillery can root up to three via Overgrowth. Others ration ordinary roots and choose Hurricane only when safely stacked.

Cat economics · how to spend the combo budget

Builders, bleeds and position

Shred is a behind-target builder and has higher weapon-damage scaling after the November change; Claw remains relevant when rear position is denied. Rake applies a bleed and supports Rend and Tear. Combo points change how much value Rip can deliver against a lasting target, but a short-lived target may make Ferocious Bite the better finish.

Predatory Discipline’s specific change is easy to overlook: Bite no longer consumes the excess Energy while still receiving additional damage based on it. That means an apparently “full Energy bite” should not be modeled with Classic’s normal excess-Energy depletion. [02] [05]

Shifting Power versus powershifting

The published Shifting Power talent at 31 Feral points instantly turns Mana into 40 Energy, costs Mana subject to shapeshift-cost discounts, has an 8-second cooldown, and cannot be used at full Energy. Furor lets Cat return with its stored Energy and adjusts for time outside Cat, except Bear prevents that regeneration. These are different mechanics; they do not imply unlimited, zero-cost Cat resets.

Natural Shapeshifter in Restoration reduces shifting Mana cost. A genuine powershift must beat the cost of a GCD, the extra Mana and any delayed combo-point action. Cat can use its dedicated talent without repeating the form animation. [02] [06]

Bear economics · threat, the pack and the healer’s life

The Lacerate contract

Lacerate is an optional one-point Feral talent unlocked at the 16-point milestone, requiring Savage Fury. It adds a high-threat bleed over 15 seconds that stacks to five and interacts with existing stacks for weapon damage. It is not a trainer spell for every Druid. The Ironhide Guardian can use it; the Clawstalker and Primal designs intentionally cannot.

With Bear’s live 115% threat bonus, Lacerate stacking and the deep Primal Bite talent, a tank can maintain target focus. But this does not grant invulnerability: defensive gear, boss facing and healer communication are still the central mechanics. [02] [06]

Threat on four enemies is not Swipe spam

Swipe strikes three enemies. The fourth requires a different answer: priority Lacerate, Faerie Fire threat, Growl when appropriate, or planned crowd control. A Bear who spends the whole pull attacking the primary target has not solved the fourth enemy; a Cat trying to imitate Swipe threat will collapse under focus.

Natural Reaction supports Rage when dodging; Thick Hide rewards Defense/armor investment. The Guard’s worst mistake is leaving Bear in order to heal while it still has boss threat. [01] [02]

Forever gear warning: the “stat-stick only” statement is outdated.

Blizzard explicitly redesigned animal-form melee auto-attacks to use your equipped weapon DPS at the form’s fixed attack speed, and weapon procs/enchantments now function in forms. Thus a higher-DPS physical weapon may improve Cat damage, while a Defense-heavy staff can protect a Bear. Neither item is automatically best for both. [01] [14]

Swiftmend needs the casting Druid’s own active HoT and does not consume it. Bloom is a different passive event at Rejuvenation/Regrowth expiry, not a detonation of Wild Growth. A dispel triggers the smaller Bloom amount. [02] [03]

Bloom makes refreshing a decision

Bloom is a Restoration talent that heals for 100% of Spirit when your Rejuvenation or Regrowth naturally ends, or 50% of Spirit when dispelled. Overwriting too early may give up an end-of-duration payoff; waiting too long can kill the target. Its value depends on both the imminent damage and actual Spirit, not merely on keeping a buff always green.

The November 6 fix made Bloom target-healing work as intended. It is a distinct mechanic from Lifebloom (whose live acquisition is not established). [02] [03]

Swiftmend ownership changes raids

Swiftmend works on a target with your own Rejuvenation or Regrowth. Other Druids’ HoTs cannot be borrowed for it; coordination is therefore required when multiple Druids are healing one tank. Official notes explicitly correct the old “consumes the effect” description: Swiftmend does not consume the qualifying HoT. [02]

The Keeper may hold it for a tank collapse; the Warden may deploy it on the single outlier who escapes Wild Growth coverage; the Guardian may use it immediately after returning from Bear.

Wild Growth · cast time, range and timing

It is not automatically instant

Wild Growth is a 31-point Restoration capstone with a 3-second base cast. Naturalist lowers cast time by up to 0.5 seconds at five ranks. As of the 10 November 2026 hotfix, Nature’s Swiftness can turn Wild Growth into an instant cast. Use that exceptional instant when multiple players are in immediate danger; otherwise evaluate whether saving Nature’s Swiftness for a tank-saving Healing Touch is more valuable. [02] [03]

It is centered on party coverage

The published tooltip heals the target and qualifying party members over seven seconds, front-loading some of the periodic healing. A party or raid spread across different elevations, distance or line of sight will not all receive equal practical value. Planning the target and group position is part of the spell’s “rotation.” [05]

Unlike a hypothetical modern raid-wide instant smart heal, this spell demands real group coordination. Do not make an entire raid’s health bar look full because one 31-point talent was pressed.

Leveling, Equipment, Endgame and PvP

Your first Cat form, your first healing assignment and your first hard boss demand different preparations.

⌘
LEVEL 1–19

The first body

Start in caster form with Wrath, Moonfire and healing. The Bear Form chain begins at level 10, Nature’s Grasp is baseline at 10, Revive at 12, Cure Poison class quest at 14 and Aquatic Form chain at 16. Bear Rage and basic dungeon tanking become possible before a full talent build.

Do not assume an early-level character can use all of the level-60 abilities in the dossiers. [01] [08]

LEVEL 20–39

The Cat and the road

The Great Cat Spirit chain unlocks Cat Form at 20; Omen of Clarity is baseline at 20. Travel Form becomes available at 30, and Poised is learned at 32. The first deep mechanics appear as you cross rows and milestone talents. A Cat can level quickly by using Energy between Mana-gated healing stops; a Bear can survive difficult pulls and tank dungeons.

Balance gains the Eclipse talent earlier than Moonkin; Restoration can achieve Swiftmend before Wild Growth. [08] [09]

LEVEL 40–60

The chosen duty

Dire Bear arrives at level 40. Dual Specialization is available at 40 from a class trainer path, making it possible to maintain separate tank/healer or caster/melee setups. You have 31 points at 40, enough for a deep 31-point capstone on a heavily committed path; level 60 yields 51 points total.

Use the post–November 2 talent tree; respecs at a major-city class trainer affect talents and potentially Legacy allocation. [04] [01]

Weapons and enchants really work in form

Blizzard’s deep dive specifically says equipped weapon DPS influences Cat and Bear autos, using form-specific attack speeds, and that enchantments, weapon procs and support effects such as Windfury work while shifted. Cat still wants the attack to land, so hit and positioning matter; Bear still needs enough survival to make threat useful. [01]

Do not lock yourself to a single “best in slot” list built from another version’s item table. Gear weight depends on current dungeon, raid phase, hit needs, specific boss dangers and whether you are tanking or attacking.

Four equipment profiles

Cat: physical hit / weapon DPS / Agility / attack power / crit where needed. Bear: stamina / armor / Defense / Dodge and enough threat statistics. Balance: spell hit / spell damage / Intellect / crit plus Mana endurance. Restoration: healing power / Spirit / Intellect and regeneration. Those are role-oriented shopping questions, not mathematically optimized stat weights.

At the time of writing, even specialist guide authors caution that level-60 stat priorities and loot sources are provisional. [13] [14]

Races and identity · four options in three families

Night Elf

Alliance Druid. Shadowmeld and Elune’s Light give stealth-positioning or offensive windows; Quickness affects movement. A natural fit for a ranged or Cat player who values repositioning, though no universal numerical superiority is claimed.

Tauren

Horde Druid. War Stomp enables a short control opening; Endurance favors survivability; Plainsrunning affects travel. Useful to think about for Bear and defensive healers, not an automatic best race.

Skyborne · High Order

Alliance Druid. Walk on Air, Wind Blessed and Read Leyline offer mobility, haste and regeneration considerations. Availability may depend on edition/pack eligibility; current racial details should be rechecked after tuning.

Horde Druid. Shares key Skyborne identity but has Skysight as a faction-side mobility racial. Choosing it can affect timing and movement more than a simple static damage expectation.

Endgame and PvP tactics

Dungeon healing and tanking

A Bear should call which target Swipe will not cover and keep the pack faced safely. A Restoration healer should prearrange HoT ownership with other Druids. Cat and Balance damage characters must know when their opener risks overtaking tank threat.

Groups benefit most when the mixed-role Druid announces a specific emergency duty before an accident forces it.

Raid assignment, not a tier list

Cat needs rear uptime and useful bleed duration. Balance needs a viable Starfire casting lane and plans the aura overlap with Leader of the Pack. Bear needs effective health and stable threat. Restoration needs known damage timing and enough Mana for the healing assignment.

Hybrid roles are tools for a raid’s roster and content, not claims that one mixed build outperforms a specialist.

PvP under pressure

Against melee, roots, Nature’s Grasp, Bear shift and Barkskin create breathing space. Against casters, Feral Charge (Bear) interrupt or careful control may change the exchange; Cat Feral Charge is NOT that interrupt. Balance casts are vulnerable to loss of distance. Healers win by staying alive, preserving a prepared HoT, and preventing a single stun from becoming a kill.

Recommended operating rule

Keep separate saved gear sets for Bear, Cat and spell/healing when you actually play those jobs. Carry suitable consumables – Forever permits most in form – but be cautious with Engineering bombs, which are specifically restricted in many form cases. Verify a current item’s stats and acquisition in game before you optimize around it. [01]

Character and combat fantasy

A Druid who intentionally holds three toolkits in mind. Deep enough for Shifting Power and the Cat version of Feral Charge, this hybrid gives up late Feral finishers and defensive talents to gain Swiftmend, Bloom, cheaper shifting and meaningful off-heals. A hybrid does not become a complete Bear tank and full DPS Cat simultaneously; it buys the right to change the problem when the group’s situation changes.

Form choice and changing shape

Default to Cat for safe damage, Bear for short physical control or a dangerous pull, caster for healing and roots. The player should name the purpose of a shift before paying for it. Furor returns stored Cat Energy (and accounts for time outside Bear); Bear interrupts that regeneration. Natural Shapeshifter reduces Mana cost, not global cooldowns or lost actions.

Mana, Energy, Rage or healing budget

This is the form-economics build: you track Mana for shifts and healing, Rage for Bear, stored Energy for Cat, and target-specific combo points. Shifting Power provides 40 Energy on cooldown but demands Mana; Natural Shapeshifter reduces that cost. Switching to Bear does not create Cat Energy while you stay there.

Weapons · armor · stat priorities

A versatile build should carry at least two distinct equipment profiles: physical weapon DPS/hit/Agility for Cat, Stamina/Defense/armor for Bear, and healing/Intellect/Spirit pieces if off-healing is an actual assignment. A single hybrid item is a convenience, not a replacement for purpose-built sets.

Defensive and utility tools

Feral Charge, Bash, Barkskin in form, Nature’s Grasp, roots and Swiftmend. The decision to abandon Bear is dangerous when still holding threat; coordinate with a second tank or choose to remain Bear.

Single target – priority not fixed rotation

  1. Open Cat when melee is viable; build just enough combo points to finish a safe target.
  2. Store enough Mana to shift; use Shifting Power to bridge a true Energy lull rather than simply resetting Cat.
  3. If incoming damage spikes, Bear shift for health/armor and a Bash or Feral Charge.
  4. When target pressure drops, leave form for Rejuvenation/Regrowth; Swiftmend on your own active effect for immediate rescue.
  5. Recover positioning and return Cat only when your next offensive sequence is worth another shift.

Four-target encounter

  1. Root one dangerous target or use Nature’s Grasp while in caster form.
  2. Bear shift to hold several attackers with Demoralizing Roar and Swipe, remembering Swipe strikes three.
  3. If an ally takes focus, exit Bear to Rejuvenation/Regrowth + Swiftmend, with the tanking handoff understood.
  4. Cat switch to chase an escaping priority target; Feral Charge (Cat) closes distance but does not interrupt.
  5. Retreat or reassign before your Mana budget is spent on too many unnecessary transitions.

Talent gates and synergies

Predatory Discipline unlocks Shifting Power at Feral 31. No Lacerate, Primal Bite, Blood Frenzy, Rend and Tear or Natural Reaction is purchased. Restoration 20 permits Swiftmend and Bloom but not the 21-point Nature’s Swiftness; that missed point is deliberate.

Leveling / late game

Start Feral; obtain the 31st Feral point and Shifting Power at the first legal opportunity, then finish the Restoration utility package. Works for questing variety; efficient sustained raid throughput is intentionally not the design goal.

Character and combat fantasy

A sustain healer whose real, verified core is Rejuvenation, Regrowth, Bloom and Swiftmend. The traditional “Lifebloom Keeper” title is used as a fantasy archetype only: a Lifebloom entry appears in Forever client spell data, but current sources do not establish an accessible trainer level, class quest, or live grant. This dossier does NOT assume Lifebloom is usable. Your job is to keep periodic care landing, end effects meaningful and an instant heal ready for the moment a prepared target falls.

Form choice and changing shape

Stay in caster/humanoid form for all real healing assignments. Cat or Bear shifting costs Mana, makes healing temporarily unavailable and can disrupt a hot-timing plan. Travel Form is useful between safe positions; use Barkskin or Nature’s Grasp for self-protection rather than gambling a critical tank moment on an unnecessary shift.

Mana, Energy, Rage or healing budget

The first resource is not just Mana but active heal durations and ownership. Bloom heals on Rejuvenation or Regrowth natural expiry for 100% of Spirit (50% when dispelled), which creates a tension between refreshing early and letting the effect finish. Reflection restores some Spirit-based regen while casting, Naturalist shortens healing casts, and a real emergency spends Mana to avoid a death.

Weapons · armor · stat priorities

Favor healing power, Intellect and Spirit (especially valuable to Bloom and regeneration), with practical mana per 5 and stamina where available. Wild Growth’s healing does not make spell damage a substitute for a healing set. Weapon DPS is not the first consideration for a primary healer.

Defensive and utility tools

Single target – priority not fixed rotation

  3. Use Swiftmend on an ally carrying YOUR active Rejuvenation or Regrowth for rapid rescue.
  4. Use Nature’s Swiftness + Healing Touch for a truly urgent single-target loss, preserving the instant tool when the tank is stable.
  5. Rebuild HoT coverage before the next planned damage window; spend Wild Growth on actual group demand, not habit.

Four-target encounter

  1. Put targeted HoTs on two or three endangered players rather than aimless blanket coverage.
  2. Let your own Rejuvenation/Regrowth run its course when Bloom’s expiration heal is likely useful; if imminent death, Swiftmend now.
  3. For clustered party damage, Wild Growth provides multi-target healing over seven seconds but begins with a cast.
  4. Use Tranquility for sustained party damage when stationary channel time is safe.
  5. Maintain distance and line of sight; a healer who survives retains future HoT ticks.

Character and combat fantasy

A deliberate raid- or party-support healer who assigns attention to simultaneous health bars rather than maximizing single-target periodic density. Form choice and changing shape

Remain caster for Wild Growth, Rejuvenation and Tranquility. Displace before group damage lands because an interrupted Wild Growth costs both time and a chance to prevent a death. Leave caster form for Travel or Bear only if personal safety demands it and a colleague can heal during the gap.

Mana, Energy, Rage or healing budget

Spend according to the number of genuinely injured nearby allies, not simply because Wild Growth is available.

Weapons · armor · stat priorities

Defensive and utility tools

Single target – priority not fixed rotation

  2. Use Swiftmend on a target with YOUR HoT already active, without stripping it.
  3. Hold Nature’s Swiftness for a hard emergency or a planned instant Wild Growth group rescue.
  4. If the fight is tank-only damage, spend cheaper GCDs carefully instead of forcing frequent group heals.

  2. Pre-position outside hazards with line of sight to the Wild Growth target.
  3. Cast Wild Growth before the whole party empties; its healing is front-loaded but spread over seven seconds.

The values below are editorial character emphasis ratings, not numerical combat performance.
