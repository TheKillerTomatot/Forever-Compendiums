The Golden Oath of Mercy and Judgment

A Paladin bears three duties beneath one oath: keep faith with the wounded, hold the ground others cannot, and answer cruelty with judgment. Each duty begins with attention, not damage.

Chapter 01 · Shared class systems

The Paladin combat engine

The Light does not choose your next action for you. Learn how Seals, Judgements, Mana and defensive timing fit together before asking whether your calling is to heal, to protect or to strike.

The oath in practice

First learn the shared combat engine. Holy, Protection and Retribution become clearer when you understand the same tools from three responsibilities: saving a life, keeping a foe occupied and ending a threat.

The seal, the judgement, the promise

A Seal is a maintained self-effect that shapes what the Paladin’s attacks do. Judgement uses the active Seal to deliver a separate effect; in Forever it does not simply consume the Seal as an old guide might suggest. After the November 13 hotfix, Judgement of Command is specifically treated as a melee attack for on-hit effects, while the other Judgements remain ranged attacks. Mana pays for spells and emergency healing. Threat is the reason a tank uses an ability on the enemy threatening the healer rather than only on the nearest foe. Learn to protect a person before optimizing a damage sequence.

Seals → weapon attacks → Judgement

In Forever, Judgement does not consume the active Seal. The November 11 hotfix made most Judgements ranged, but the November 13 correction restores Judgement of Command as a melee attack that can activate on-hit effects; other Judgements remain ranged. Older blanket descriptions are no longer accurate.

Chapter 02 · The hand of salvation

Holy: The Art of Answering Wounds

Holy begins with the wounded person, not the spellbar. Learn how soon healing must land, what Mana can be spared and when a Paladin may safely add offense without abandoning a life entrusted to them.

What a healer watches

A Holy Paladin watches both the injured ally and the next expected hit. A large heal has little value if it finishes too late; a smaller efficient heal may be better when damage is predictable. Mana matters over the entire dungeon, not just one pull. When fighting solo, offensive Seals and Judgements help turn defense into progress; in a party, changing targets to save a fragile ally is often the right use of a global cooldown. Healing talents add options to this baseline kit, but their presence should always be checked against the actual build.

HOLY · TRIAGE

The endangered ally

A healing Paladin watches the next dangerous hit and the time required for a heal to land. Reserve sufficient Mana for the crisis instead of treating every missing point of health as equally urgent.

HOLY · OPPORTUNITY

When offense can safely help

Holy damage has a place when immediate healing is covered. Every attack still spends time and resources that might be needed to restore an ally, so reassess when the battlefield changes.

HOLY · RESILIENCE

Healing under pressure

A Paladin in the thick of an encounter must protect casting space and survive stray attacks. Position, armor and defensive cooldowns are part of the healing plan.

Field test: the healer’s second duty

A dungeon ally loses a little Health while the tank is about to receive a heavy attack. Do not spend your last reliable cast window topping the safer player merely because that bar is incomplete. Choose the heal that can arrive before the true danger, then decide whether there is room to attack again.

Chapter 03 · The living fortress

Protection: Holding the Line

Protection is the promise that an enemy will not be free to choose a weaker victim. Build threat, wear the shield when its protections are required, and treat predictable survival as an active part of your duty.

Holding the line is a group task

A tank’s first job is not to take the most damage: it is to keep dangerous enemies attacking the character equipped to survive them. Threat is that attraction; aggro is the enemy choosing whom to attack. Righteous Fury and appropriate Holy attacks help establish it, while a taunt-style rescue ability solves an immediate target problem without permanently replacing steady threat. Face enemies away from vulnerable party members and watch for new arrivals. Spending all available Mana on a harmless pack can leave you with nothing when a second group appears.

PROTECTION · THREAT

Holding the front line

Protection is a commitment to enemy attention, shield use and predictable survival. The question is whether the next attacker stays on you, not whether your personal damage looks impressive.

PROTECTION · TEMPO

Threat through decisive action

An aggressive tank can use active offense to keep an enemy engaged, but the shield and healer remain central. Spend Mana on meaningful control instead of chasing every damage opportunity.

PROTECTION · SUPPORT

Support without surrendering the pull

A Paladin may bring more than one kind of utility, but cannot neglect the task the party has assigned. Secure enemy control first; aid allies when it does not leave them exposed.

Field test: the loose enemy

The group is attacking your first target when another foe starts toward the healer. Keep the shield and any shield-dependent benefits in mind, regain the second enemy’s attention and avoid spending all Mana on the first target’s damage. A tank’s success is shared safety.

Chapter 04 · The divine executioner

Retribution: weapon, spell and support

Retribution weighs the heavy strike against the spell and the obligation to the group. A Seal, a Judgement and a utility action each claim time and Mana; choosing well matters more than chasing an uninterrupted damage string.

A weapon in service of judgment

Retribution blends weapon contact with Holy effects, so a missed approach or a forced retreat can disrupt the entire sequence. Decide which Seal and Judgement support the fight rather than treating every button as an unconditional cooldown. An on-hit effect is a secondary event triggered by an eligible attack; the November 13 rules make Judgement of Command an exception to the generally ranged Judgement classification. A two-handed Paladin can still support an ally with a timely heal or blessing, but that choice consumes Mana and time that could have been spent attacking.

RETRIBUTION · WEAPON

The commitment of a heavy weapon

A two-handed attack can be decisive, but its timing and the target’s movement matter. Pair pressure with the right Seal and Judgement rather than assuming weapon size solves every fight.

RETRIBUTION · SPELLWORK

Weapon and spell in counterpoint

Holy and weapon attacks share a combat rhythm shaped by Mana and cooldowns. Choose the next action because the target and timing warrant it, not to alternate attacks for their own sake.

RETRIBUTION · DUTY

The strength of an ally

A damage-focused Paladin still brings Blessings, Auras and emergency intervention. Understanding when to protect the group is part of Retribution, not an admission that the fight has gone wrong.

Field test: judgment or rescue

An enemy is nearly defeated when an ally is suddenly threatened. Finish the foe only if doing so is actually the safest answer; a Blessing, a defensive tool or an emergency heal may buy the party more than another swing. Your oath includes that choice.

Chapter 05 · The pilgrimage

Leveling, gear, races and party compositions

15 November addendum · Dungeon XP

Blizzard clarified a November 14 adjustment to dungeon experience: XP nullification now applies when a higher-level party member is more than 12 levels above the enemies. The updated rule applies to newly started instances; a dungeon already in progress when the change deployed was not retroactively updated. Official development clarification.

Leveling, gear and party composition matter at least as much as the final talent split. Forever rewards keeping an offensive set and a group-role set rather than trying to make one outfit do everything.

From first quest to first dungeon

In solo leveling, being able to heal between attacks can reduce recovery time, but it does not mean Mana is infinite. Gear for your current job: a weapon for a damage-focused adventure, suitable defense for tanking, and healing-oriented attributes when assigned to keep others alive. In a party, tell the group whether you intend to heal, tank, or deal damage; a hybrid talent layout does not automatically confer equal strength in all three jobs. When a fight goes wrong, use utility to keep the party alive before judging the rotation by its damage numbers.

The 1–60 pilgrimage

1–9 Learn Seals and Judgements; Holy Strike arrives at 6.
10–19 Talent points, Righteous Fury and Seal of Fury establish early identity.
20–29 Consecration becomes baseline at 20; early talent specialization develops.

Dual specialization at level 40

Level 40 · 50 gold

Purchase a second talent specialization from a class trainer. It enables the same character to maintain a damage build and a heal/tank role, but it does not magically create a second equipment set.

Useful paired identities

  • Healing for groups ↔ two-handed solo play
  • Defensive tanking ↔ proactive threat generation
  • Offensive Holy ↔ dedicated healing
  • Hybrid support ↔ dedicated healing
  • Heavy-weapon pressure ↔ utility-minded Retribution

How a party composition changes blessings

Physical-heavy group

Might and broad-stat bonuses often have more value. Control AoE so it does not break planned crowd control.

Caster-heavy group

Mana support and resistance auras can matter more. Hold threat against early ranged burst and do not overuse expensive AoE.

Paladin as healer

Oath on the tank, efficient direct heals, timely Cleanse, Freedom and Protection. Prepare Light’s Vigil on the party actually taking damage.

The verdict
