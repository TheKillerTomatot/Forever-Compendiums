Chapter 00 / The Aether Archives

The Opening Spell

Before a Mage learns to command the elements, they must learn to command the next cast. Distance, Mana and a safe window determine whether a spell becomes power—or an interrupted gesture.

The first lesson

Study Mana and casting safety before considering elaborate talent combinations. Arcane, Fire and Frost are taught here as distinct disciplines with shared responsibilities: control the enemy, manage the fight’s length and leave yourself a way out.

Before the spell takes flight

The Mage is powerful precisely because a cast is not guaranteed to finish. You stand where enemies take time to reach you, use control to create that time, and watch Mana before the encounter becomes an escape. A new player can begin with a simple rhythm: pull from range, use a suitable damage spell, slow or root an approaching enemy, then move to a safer position. Fire, Frost, and Arcane are not just three colors of damage; each asks what you can afford to cast when the enemy starts answering back.

The long equation

Mana is time

Arcane's new spell-damage stacks amplify every follow-up, but increasingly expensive Blasts can bankrupt a long encounter. A skilled Mage decides when not to stack.

The sudden spark

Crit is an opening

Fire's Heating Up is a cast-time reduction system, not an automatic instant Pyroblast. Fire Blast can create progress even without a critical strike.

The absent winter

Frozen is a rule

Frost can immobilize a target with a real root, or use Fingers of Frost to qualify spells for Frozen-target bonuses without immobilizing the target at all.

Chapter 01 / The Aether Archives

The Three Pillars of Spellcraft

Every cast asks three questions: how much Mana remains, which proc window is open, and how long will the enemy be controlled – or allowed to cast?

Three schools, one survival problem

Mana is both your ammunition and your reserve for control. A rotation is a repeatable priority, not a command to ignore danger. Frostbolt can slow approach, Frost Nova can root nearby enemies, and Blink can help create distance; neither a slow nor a root is guaranteed to affect every target. Counterspell interrupts an eligible spell cast, which can matter more than one extra damaging cast. Polymorph removes a suitable enemy from the fight temporarily, so avoid breaking it with area damage. The first Mage skill is choosing which threat deserves attention.

Pillar I · Mana

The aether reservoir

Intellect increases the pool, Spirit contributes regeneration, and mp5 restores Mana independently of cast pauses. Arcane Meditation can retain part of Spirit-based regeneration while casting. An Arcane Blast chain is a series of financial commitments.

Pillar II · Procs

The unstable clock

Arcane Concentration makes a subsequent damaging spell free after a proc. Missile Barrage offers free compressed Arcane Missiles; Heating Up accelerates the next Pyroblast; Fingers of Frost enables two spells to treat their target as Frozen.

Pillar III · Control

The protected cast

Frostbolt Chill, a true Frost Nova root, Polymorph, Counterspell and Blink create the time necessary to cast. These are different jobs: a slow changes pace, a root stops movement, silence interrupts casting, and a shield mitigates damage.

Chapter 02 / The Aether Archives

Arcane · The Aether Laboratory

Arcane has acquired its own rotation logic. Its new identity is not just an old cooldown added to a Frostbolt turret: the 18-second Arcane Blast spell-power window changes the value of every follow-up spell.

Power with a receipt

Arcane-focused builds ask you to recognize when a costly cast is justified. Spell damage can rise while the Mana available for later control and escapes declines; do not confuse the largest immediate number with the safest fight. Before using Arcane Blast repeatedly, identify the available cast window and the next movement requirement. A mana-management talent changes the cost calculation only for a character that actually bought it. For a beginner in a dungeon, staying alive and having Mana ready for a dangerous caster often contributes more than squeezing in one extra spell.

ARCANE · BURST

The burst window

Arcane Power and Arcane Blast can concentrate damage into a costly interval when the relevant talents and spells are available. Know the enemy’s remaining lifetime and the price of the next cast before extending the sequence.

ARCANE · ENDURANCE

The Mana ledger

Not every moment should be spent at maximum output. Cheaper filler, regeneration and pauses between pulls can preserve enough power for the encounter that actually matters.

ARCANE · ADAPTATION

Learning across schools

Arcane and elemental spells offer different timing, critical-strike and Mana considerations. Cross-school choices reward understanding the shared casting rules rather than memorizing a single fixed sequence.

Spell physics / S02 · S03 · S05

Arcane Blast is a window, not a reset loop

Each successful cast adds a stack of Spell Damage based on the Mage's Intellect. Up to four stacks share the same expiration measured from the first cast. The next Blast becomes more expensive; a different damage spell can use the spell-power gain without clearing it. That makes Arcane Wrath, Frostbolt or Fireball valid finishers of a setup when trained and worth casting.

Arcane Wrath is the 16-point talent: 3.5-second cast, six-second cooldown and additional Spell Damage calculated from 5% of maximum Mana; Fire's Accelerant can shorten its cast. Only the Annihilator and Spellweaver – and the Savant's 17 Arcane points – have it in the proposed allocations.

Proc interaction / S02 · S03

Two kinds of free

Arcane Concentration can make the next damaging spell cost no Mana after a damage spell hits. Missile Barrage compresses and makes Arcane Missiles free when it procs. With both active, Barrage's Missiles channel does not consume Clearcasting, and Barrage is spent at the start of that channel.

That does not prove that every possible sequence is DPS-optimal: the correct cast depends on time remaining in the Arcane Blast window, whether you can stand still for the channel, and how much Mana the boss still requires.

Chapter 03 / The Aether Archives

Fire · The Pyromancer’s Forge

Fire is the discipline of dangerous commitment. A critical strike may open an exciting next cast, but a spell that never completes is only lost time and Mana. Read the life of the target, the safety of your position and the space the flames will occupy.

Fire rewards preparation

Burning effects and high-impact casts matter most if the enemy stays alive and in range long enough. A damage-over-time effect keeps hurting after the spell lands; refreshing it prematurely may reduce its value. Critical strikes are attacks that deal extra damage, while procs are effects that trigger under particular conditions. A Fire build may plan around both, but cannot assume a proc will arrive on demand. When several enemies approach, secure control or space before using area attacks: Flamestrike rewards enemies staying in one location, not a Mage standing still while surrounded.

FIRE · CAST TIMING

Preparing the heavy flame

Long casts and accelerated opportunities create very different attack windows. Watch your procs, the enemy’s movement and the danger of committing to a spell you may not finish.

FIRE · AREA PRESSURE

Fire spread with intention

Damage over time and nearby-target spells ask a different question from single-target burst: will the enemy stay alive and close enough for the effect to matter?

FIRE · POSITION

The changing rhythm of a burn

Fire rewards commitment when targets remain exposed but punishes a Mage who stands still after the battlefield has changed. Use shorter opportunities to prepare the next safe cast.

Ignite and Scorch are personal

Forever's class deep dive makes each Fire Mage's Ignite and Improved Scorch vulnerability its own contribution rather than a single pooled raid effect. The result: two Fire Mages on the same boss maintain different personal pressure. When a boss dies quickly, five Scorch stacks may not repay their setup; when it survives, Scorch's lasting Fire vulnerability can matter.

Combustion increases critical chance as Fire hits land, stopping after three qualifying non-periodic Fire crits. Your ability to remain in casting range changes the value of the cooldown.

AoE is a positional rotation

Flamestrike wants enemies to remain in a location. Blast Wave dazes and buys space at the Mage's position. Frost Nova immobilizes but can be broken by damage. Improved Blizzard slows in Forever at 40% (talented), below the older Classic assumption of 65%.

A Fire Mage's area-damage opening depends on tank grouping and safer spell placement; a single-target Fireball order may look familiar even when the crucial pack decisions differ completely.

Frost · The Glacial Sanctum

Frost is the art of making time. Slows, roots and Frozen opportunities have distinct consequences: one delays pursuit, another controls a moment, and neither absolves the Mage from planning the next safe cast.

Control has different shapes

A slow changes how quickly an opponent closes distance; a root stops movement for its duration; an immunity can ignore either one. Frost Nova is a root, not a guarantee of safety: damage, including certain procs and Blizzard damage under the November 13 fix, can break it. Blizzard can punish groups in an area but requires positioning and an appropriate channel window. Watch the enemies at the edge of the spell, because one survivor leaving the pack may determine the next Blink. Even in a damage-heavy build, control is an expenditure of time wisely purchased.

FROST · CONTROL

Slowing the battle

Frost protects casting time by making an enemy’s approach more difficult. The objective is not merely to deal cold damage: it is to decide where the next exchange happens.

FROST · PRECISION

Recognizing the frozen window

Certain Frost effects reward attacks against Frozen targets. Learn which effects establish that condition and when it will end, rather than assuming every slow counts as a freeze.

FROST · SURVIVAL

Safety while moving

A resilient Mage balances defenses, instant opportunities and control. The longer you survive, the more valuable deliberate positioning becomes compared with one rushed cast.

Positioning laboratory · actually Frozen vs virtually Frozen

A target can be slowed by a Chill, immobilized by Frost Nova or Frostbite, or merely treated as Frozen for the spell's damage calculation by Fingers of Frost. These should not be conflated.

Rooted target: movement is stopped if the root holds. Frozen-target bonuses may apply.

Rules illustration, not a simulation of diminishing returns or projectile collisions. Fingers has two charges and lasts 15 s at the verified rank.

Control toolkit ≠ damage toolkit

Frostbolt Chill reduces approach speed, Frost Nova can create a true root, Blizzard deals area damage and has a talented limited slow, and Ice Block prevents damage at the price of action. None of these guarantees that a raid boss can be immobilized.

Shatter raises crit chance against Frozen targets; Ice Lance gains extra damage against them. Fingers of Frost confers the Frozen-target condition to a limited number of spells without adding a root.

Frozen opportunities change priorities

Frost talents can emphasize damage against Frozen targets or a safer sequence of resets and defensive spells. In one case, a proc changes the next spell choice; in the other, positioning and protection matter most: how close is the enemy, what slow is active and where can Blink take you?

Forever Ice Barrier now rewards sustained Frost casting with a timed Frost damage buff. Winter's Chill is reworked for Cone of Cold setup; do not assign a permanent generic crit vulnerability.

Shared cooldown warning: Ice Lance and Fire Blast share an eight-second cooldown under the current Forever rules. A split Fire/Frost Mage cannot assume both instants are independently available in a simultaneous burst sequence. S02 · S03

Field test: what counts as Frozen

A slowed enemy is approaching, and a talent rewards attacks against Frozen targets. Check whether the target is actually Frozen or merely moving more slowly. Use control to create a safe attack window and avoid spending a high-value spell on an assumed condition that has not occurred.

Equipment is a question of time

Intellect: pool size, critical-strike contribution and stronger Arcane Blast spell damage through the talent's Intellect conversion. Spirit: regeneration becomes most useful when casts stop or when a purchased effect permits continued regeneration. Mana per five seconds: can support long encounters even while casting.

Spell damage: improves spells in proportion to their Forever coefficients – do not reuse an old generic coefficient spreadsheet blindly. Hit: prevents missed casts and lost procs; Precision (Frost row 1, 3/3) grants hit to all spell schools. Crit: feeds Ignite, Heating Up and Shatter opportunities where applicable. Stamina: keeps a Mage alive through mistakes and PvP pressure.

School-specific power may favor a pure spec, while general spell damage is safer for Frostfire hybrids. There is no authenticated single best-in-slot gear list in this manuscript; items and phase unlocks change.

Why the Mana bar tells a different story

Spirit-based regeneration is traditionally constrained by the five-second rule after spending Mana; Mage Armor and Arcane Meditation can let a portion continue during casting. The exact realized rate depends on live formula, gear and buffs; avoid importing one Classic coefficient as settled Forever fact.

Spell rank selection: a low-rank attack can save Mana, but it may lose base damage and change spell-power coefficient. Blizzard's Nov notes retain a specific downranking penalty for Blizzard triggering certain procs with ranks learned more than 12 levels below the Mage's level; do not infer an identical penalty for every spell.

Drink after difficult pulls; use conjured water, a wand for safe low-health finishing, appropriate mana potions when worth the cooldown and noncombat preparation before a serious boss.

The magical utility satchel

Control

Polymorph, Nova, slows

Polymorph separates a dangerous target; avoid breaking it with Blast Wave, Blizzard or Flamestrike. Frost Nova and Frostbite are real roots subject to target immunities and proc-root diminishing returns. Chill is only a slow. Cone of Cold was shortened in Forever.

Cast denial

Counterspell

Counterspell is an interrupt and school-lock tool, not merely a damage spell. Improved Counterspell in Arcane can add silence, but the documented builds later in this book do not purchase it. Do not assign a four-second talent silence to their base Counterspell.

Escape

Blink, shields, Ice Block

Blink is a position change. Mana Shield pays Mana to reduce damage; Ice Barrier requires deep Frost here. Ice Block is talent-gated, not a baseline button for the Arcane Annihilator, Mana Architect or the Fire Savant.

Party services

Food, water, Intellect

Conjure Food and Water reduce party downtime; Arcane Intellect and Arcane Brilliance support casters. Remove Lesser Curse is utility. Portals and teleports improve group travel, but check the trainer and destination availability on the current spellbook.

Mystery scrolls

Comprehension

A Forever-specific Mage minor tradeskill reads decipherable drops through Comprehend Scroll. This is a distinct utility path, not a guaranteed direct-damage buff or a claim about every scroll outcome.

Trades and preparation

Professions & consumables

Tailoring can equip a cloth caster and supply bags; Enchanting improves suitable gear; Herbalism/Alchemy can support potion and elixir preparation. Use current recipe and phase availability, not an assumed later-expansion crafting list.

Races in the live class matrix

Alliance: Human, Gnome and High Order Skyborne. Horde: Orc, Troll and Undead. Orc Mage and Alliance Skyborne Mage are Forever additions. Race passives and active effects have undergone their own balance passes.

For a numerical ranking, verify current racial tooltip and character statistics, then test against your gear and encounter. This book avoids making unverified race-DPS claims. A playable race and a reliable crowd-control racial may matter more than a tiny damage estimate. S12 · S15

Dual Specialization & trainer practice

Forever makes Dual Specialization available from level 40; talents are changed at a class trainer in a major city. Consider keeping one efficient solo/quest profile and one group PvE or PvP profile. Do not pretend this restores all expended Mana or ignores spell school lockouts.

A broadly forgiving leveling Mage often values Frostbolt control earlier than a fully realized Arcane burn or Fire/Frost crit laboratory. Build identity changes as major talent spells and proper spell ranks unlock.

Endgame evidence limit: no fixed level-60 damage numbers, exact hit cap, encounter-specific BiS or simulation-derived ranking is claimed. Gear and future raid tuning can reorder expected strengths. Source notes were checked to 13 November 2026; a later hotfix may invalidate details.
